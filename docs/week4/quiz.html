Week 4 self-check — comparisons and fitted lines

Use the stated design, units, and direction before interpreting output. These questions are formative and need no new statistical method. Refer to the statistics reference as needed.

1. Interpret the direction and interval

For independent seedlings, the estimated treated-minus-control mean growth difference is 6.22 mm, with a 95% confidence interval from 2.70 to 9.73 mm. What quantity is this interval describing? Is it a range for 95% of the plants?

Answer

It describes uncertainty in the population mean difference, under the model assumptions and in the stated treated-minus-control direction. It is not a range containing 95% of individual plants. A 95% confidence procedure covers the fixed true quantity in about 95% of repeated samples when its assumptions hold.

2. What does the p-value condition on?

Does a p-value of 0.0015 mean a 0.15% probability that the null hypothesis is true, or prove that a 6 mm difference matters biologically?

Answer

Neither. It describes the probability, under the null model and its assumptions, of a test statistic at least as extreme as observed. It is not the probability of the hypothesis. Biological importance needs the effect’s size, units, uncertainty, organism, and purpose. The test cannot repair dependent units.

3. Pair by identity

The same ten plants were measured before and after increasing light. Why should the analysis use within-plant differences? Could independently sorting the two measurement columns improve their pairing?

Answer

The two readings belong to each same plant, so calculate after minus before within matching IDs. There are ten independent pairs, not twenty independent plants. Sorting columns independently destroys that identity and does not improve pairing. The fixed before/after order also leaves a time/order limitation.

4. Invert the calibration in the stated direction

The fitted line is approximately absorbance = 0.03957 + 0.06489 × concentration_mg_l. For an in-range absorbance of 0.310, what formula estimates concentration?

Worked answer

Rearrange to concentration = (0.310 - 0.03957) / 0.06489, giving about 4.17 mg/L. Use full fitted coefficients in code. We fitted absorbance as the measured response to known concentration; reversing the regression is a different fit. The inverse calculation is a point estimate, not full calibration uncertainty.

5. A diluted reading is new evidence

An unknown’s original reading is above the measured standards. Can you simply report the line’s extrapolated concentration? If a new in-range reading gives 7.40 mg/L after 1.00 mL was diluted to 10.00 mL total, what is the original estimate?

Answer and check

Flag the original as outside range rather than treat extrapolation as a supported result. A new in-range diluted measurement permits a stated correction: 7.40 × (10.00 / 1.00) ≈ 74.0 mg/L, using rounded numbers. The dilution assumes no measured material was lost or reacted. It does not validate another old out-of-range reading without its own new measurement.

6. Slope units and causal limits

A fitted dry-mass-versus-leaf-area slope is about 4.11. Dry mass is in mg and area in cm². What are the slope’s units, and what claim is not established by this observational fit?

Answer

The units are mg per cm²: fitted change in dry mass for a one-cm² increase in area. The sample association does not establish that experimentally changing area would cause this mass change. Inspect residuals and the measured range, and avoid extrapolating far beyond observed leaf areas.

7. Reversing the contrast

A treated-minus-control95% interval is2.70 to9.73 mm. What is the control-minus-treated interval? What happens to the two-sided p-value?

Answer and reasoning

The reversed interval is−9.73 to−2.70 mm. Reverse the endpoint order as well as their signs. The two-sided p-value is unchanged because the equal-means question and observations are the same. Clearly label the reported direction.

8. Different confidence levels, same observations

With identical data and model, would a99% interval normally be narrower or wider than a90% interval? Does changing the interval level alter the observed means?

Answer and reasoning

The99% procedure gives the wider interval to target greater long-run coverage. The sample means do not change. Do not select a confidence level after seeing which one gives a preferred conclusion; retain the stated analysis.

9. What if the smaller study has a narrower interval?

The transfer study has 9 plants per group instead of 12, yet its interval is narrower. Must R have failed?

Answer and reasoning

No. Interval width also depends on observed variability and model assumptions. The transfer study has lower observed group spread. When several features differ, sample size alone does not determine the ordering of interval widths.

10. Pairing checked only by means

Independently sorting before and after columns preserves their separate means. Does that validate the paired analysis?

Answer and reasoning

No. It destroys subject identity and can alter the spread of within-subject changes while preserving their average. In the actual ten-plant file the correct and artificial pairings have the same mean change but very different SDs and intervals. Sort complete rows together or match by recorded identity.

11. A new instrument gives two unknown readings

Transfer standards cover measured signals0.024–0.525. X1 reads0.270 and X2 reads0.700. Which gets an inverse estimate, and what should happen to the other record?

Answer and reasoning

X1 is within the measured range and estimates about 2.48 mg/L using the new fitted coefficients. X2 remains in the output with its reading, an out-of-range flag, and no supported concentration estimate. A new suitable measurement is required; do not delete its ID or invent dilution evidence.

12. A unit conversion changes a slope

A mass-on-area slope is4.11 mg/cm². Area is converted to mm² by multiplying by 100. What happens to slope and fitted masses?

Answer and reasoning

The slope becomes about 0.0411 mg/mm², a factor of 100 smaller numerically. The intercept and fitted masses remain the same apart from rounding. The biological association did not become weaker merely because the predictor unit changed. A line through formula-calculated values is also not independent empirical evidence.
