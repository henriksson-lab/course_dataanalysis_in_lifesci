Day 13 — Summarising groups without hiding the plants

Lecture slides · Quiz · Weekly revision

Today’s question

A bar chart of two group means cannot tell a reader how many plants are behind each bar, how much they varied, or whether one unusual plant pulled a mean up. Today you make a small table with one row per group and a figure that shows every plant together with its group mean, so a reader gets both.

Start day13-groups.R in the project root:

library(readr)
library(dplyr)
library(ggplot2)
seedlings <- read_csv("data/seedlings.csv", show_col_types = FALSE) |>
  mutate(growth_mm = final_mm - initial_mm)

Save today: day13-groups.R, output/growth-summary.csv and output/growth-summary.png.

One row per group

Use group_by() and summarise() to find out the number of plants, mean, median and SD of growth in each treatment group:

group_summary <- seedlings |>
  group_by(treatment) |>
  summarise(n_plants = n(),
            n_observed = sum(!is.na(growth_mm)),
            mean_mm = mean(growth_mm, na.rm = TRUE),
            median_mm = median(growth_mm, na.rm = TRUE),
            sd_mm = sd(growth_mm, na.rm = TRUE),
            .groups = "drop")
group_summary

group_by(treatment) makes the next step work separately within each treatment, and summarise() returns one row per group. The names left of = become the column names. n() counts rows, that is plant records, while sum(!is.na(growth_mm)) counts the growth values that are actually there (! means “not”). .groups = "drop" returns an ordinary table.

treatment n_plants n_observed mean_mm median_mm sd_mm
control 12 12 15.28 15.65 3.19
treated 12 12 21.49 20.85 4.87

group_summary has two rows that describe groups; seedlings still has the 24 plants. You need both: the plants for the figure, the summary for the means.

Plants and means in one figure

group_plot <- ggplot(seedlings, aes(x = treatment, y = growth_mm)) +
  geom_point(position = position_jitter(width = 0.08, height = 0, seed = 13),
             alpha = 0.75) +
  geom_point(data = group_summary, aes(x = treatment, y = mean_mm),
             inherit.aes = FALSE, shape = 18, size = 4, colour = "darkred") +
  labs(x = "Treatment", y = "Change in seedling length (mm)",
       caption = "Small dots: individual plants. Red diamonds: group means.") +
  theme_minimal()
group_plot

The first geom_point() draws one dot per plant from seedlings. position_jitter(width = 0.08, height = 0) moves dots a little sideways so they do not hide each other, but never up or down, so every dot stays at its measured growth. The second geom_point() takes its data from group_summary and draws the means as red diamonds; inherit.aes = FALSE tells it to use its own x and y instead of looking for growth_mm, which the summary table does not have.

The figure shows what the table cannot: the groups overlap, one treated plant grew only 12.2 mm, and two treated plants grew about 30 mm. If you add whiskers, say what they show. Mean ± SD describes how plants vary; a confidence interval, which comes in week 4, describes how uncertain a mean is. Points and means without whiskers are a good choice for today.

Histograms

A histogram cuts the growth axis into intervals, called bins, and counts the plants in each. Use one to find out the shape of each group’s growth values:

ggplot(seedlings, aes(x = growth_mm)) +
  geom_histogram(binwidth = 4, boundary = 0, colour = "white") +
  facet_wrap(~ treatment, ncol = 1) +
  labs(x = "Change in seedling length (mm)", y = "Number of plants") +
  theme_minimal()

facet_wrap(~ treatment, ncol = 1) draws one panel per group, stacked on the same growth axis so the panels can be compared. With 4 mm bins, most control plants fall between 12 and 20 mm, and most treated plants between 20 and 24 mm, with two treated plants in the 28–32 mm bin. With only 12 plants per group the shape changes noticeably when you change the bin width.

Saving the table and the figure

write_csv(group_summary, "output/growth-summary.csv")
ggsave("output/growth-summary.png", plot = group_plot, width = 6, height = 4)

The CSV holds the numbers and the PNG holds the picture; until you save them, both exist only in R’s memory. Find the two files in your file manager and open them outside RStudio.

Group summaries for the seedlings

  1. Use filter() and mean() to find out whether the control mean in group_summary matches the plants it came from. You should get 15.28 mm.
  2. Use the same summary code on output/seedlings-cleaned.csv from day 11 (add growth_mm if it is not there) to find out how the missing P06 shows up. Control has n_plants 12 but n_observed 11, and a mean of 15.21 mm instead of 15.28 mm, because a different set of plants contributed. This is why the table keeps both counts.
  3. Change the histogram’s binwidth to 2 and then to 6 and find out which features stay the same. At every width the treated values sit higher than the controls and the two treated plants near 30 mm stand apart; smaller peaks and gaps appear or vanish with the bin width, so do not describe them.
  4. Write a caption for output/growth-summary.png that a reader could check without the code: the unit (one plant), the outcome and its unit, what dots and diamonds mean, and n. For example: “Seven-day growth (mm) of 24 individually potted seedlings, 12 per group. Dots are plants; red diamonds are group means.”

Another lab: percentage yield

Your teacher may choose this instead of task 2 above. In data/organic-recovery.csv each row is one independent synthesis run. Calculate yield_percent = 100 * isolated_mass_g / theoretical_mass_g, as on day 12, then use group_by(protocol) and summarise() to find out the number of runs, mean yield and SD for each protocol. Both protocols have six runs; the standard mean is 68.67% (SD 4.32 percentage points) and the modified mean 77.00% (SD 3.74). Plot the runs with a mean marker as above, with the y-axis labelled “Isolated product yield (%)”, and save output/organic-yield-summary.csv and output/organic-yield-summary.png.

Check your understanding

Why show individual dots as well as the means?

The dots show how many plants there are, how much they vary, where unusual values lie and how much the groups overlap. A higher mean does not mean every treated plant grew more than every control plant.

The summary table has two rows. Are they two new observations?

No. Each row describes a group of 12 plants. The 24 plants stay in seedlings, and that is the table any later analysis uses.
